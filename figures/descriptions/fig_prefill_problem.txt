The Pre-fill Memory Problem: Full vs Chunked

IMPORTANT: Pure white background (#FFFFFF). No gray tint, no colored background, no gradient. The diagram must have a clean white background suitable for an academic paper/blog post.

This diagram compares two approaches to processing a long prompt: standard (one big forward pass) vs chunked pre-fill. Layout is two columns, left and right.

LEFT COLUMN - "Standard Pre-fill":
- Title: "Standard: One Forward Pass" in bold.
- A large square representing the attention matrix for an 8-token prompt.
- The square is 8x8 with the full lower triangle filled in pastel red/pink (indicating high memory usage).
- Label inside: "(8, 8) attention matrix"
- Label below: "Peak memory: O(n^2)"
- A warning icon or text: "Memory spike!"
- Arrow pointing to the large square: "Must materialize the ENTIRE attention matrix at once"

RIGHT COLUMN - "Chunked Pre-fill":
- Title: "Chunked: Window-Sized Pieces" in bold.
- Three smaller squares representing chunks:
  - Chunk 1: a 3x3 attention matrix, pastel green fill. Label: "Chunk 1: tokens 0-2"
  - Chunk 2: a 3x3 attention matrix, pastel green fill. Label: "Chunk 2: tokens 3-5"
  - Chunk 3: a 2x2 attention matrix, pastel green fill. Label: "Chunk 3: tokens 6-7"
- The three chunks are arranged vertically with arrows between them showing sequential processing.
- Label below all chunks: "Peak memory: O(w^2)"
- A checkmark icon or text: "Low, constant memory"
- Arrow: "Process one chunk at a time, each only w x w"

BETWEEN COLUMNS:
- An arrow or "vs" label.

BOTTOM COMPARISON:
- "8K tokens, standard: ~4,160 MB peak memory"
- "8K tokens, chunked (w=4096): ~1,056 MB peak memory"
- "74.6% reduction in peak memory"

Style: Clean academic illustration with sans-serif labels, pastel red for the large (expensive) matrix, pastel green for the small (efficient) chunks. The size contrast should make the memory savings visually obvious. White background throughout.
